25 questions to ask your girlfriend

25 questions · Updated November 2026

Most of us are better at planning dinner than at asking something new. These questions are easy to ask and hard to answer with just "good." Start with the ones that make you smile and see where she takes it.

Warm up

  1. What is a tiny thing that always makes your day better?
  2. What is your comfort show, the one you can watch on repeat?
  3. What is the best meal you have ever had?
  4. If you could live anywhere for a year, where would it be?
  5. What did you want to be when you were ten?
  6. What is something you are weirdly good at?
  7. What song takes you right back to being a teenager?

About us

  1. What is your favorite memory of us so far?
  2. What is something I do that makes you feel really seen?
  3. How do you like me to show I love you? Words, time, a little surprise?
  4. What is one thing you wish we did more of?
  5. When do you feel closest to me?
  6. What is something about us you hope never changes?
  7. What is a place you want us to see together?

Deeper

  1. What is something you are working on in yourself right now?
  2. What was a hard time that made you who you are?
  3. What do you need more of when you are having a rough week?
  4. What does a really good life look like to you in five years?
  5. What is a fear you have never said out loud?
  6. Who in your life shaped you the most?

Flirty

  1. What is the best compliment anyone has ever given you?
  2. What is something I do that you secretly find irresistible?
  3. What would a perfect night in with me look like?
  4. If I planned a surprise date, what would you hope it was?
  5. What is the first thing you thought when we kissed?

A tip before you start

Ask, then be quiet for a second longer than feels comfortable. Most people fill the silence with the real answer. And answer your own question too. It is a conversation, not an interview.

Questions people ask

What are good questions to ask a girlfriend you have been with for years?

Go for the ones about the future and about how she wants to be loved now. People change, and the answer you knew three years ago may not be the one today.

What if she does not like talking about feelings?

Start with the fun group and let it get deeper by itself. Keep it light, and never push for an answer.
